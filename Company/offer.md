## The three-tier sales model (this is the actual decision rule — apply it before quoting anything)

**Tier A — Publish Now.** Deliverable from a clear template in 1-2 days: one-page/landing sites, website refresh, spreadsheet cleanup, SOPs, social/graphic batches, presentation cleanup, research briefs, Google Business content. Quote from the catalog's starter range directly. Low trust bar, fast close.

**Tier B — Sell After 2-3 Demos.** Needs more setup, testing, or revision judgment: 3-5 page sites, ecommerce setup, monthly content packages, KPI dashboards, CRM cleanup, lead-magnet funnels, chatbots. Quote after a short discovery conversation.

**Tier C — Audit First, Never Quote Blind.** Credentials, security, integrations, or production risk involved: RAG/private-document AI, API integrations, production automations, custom internal tools, migrations, local/private AI infrastructure, security-sensitive or regulated-data work. Always requires a paid discovery/audit phase before a firm quote — this is also where Build, Retrofit, and Husks-scoped work lives today.

## Productized bundles (fixed packages, catalog-priced)

1. **Local Business Launch** — $750-$1,500: one-page site, copy polish, contact form, Google Business content, analytics, handoff.
2. **Content Engine** — $350-$1,200/mo: one source item turned into posts, newsletter, graphics, and short clips with approval.
3. **Operations Cleanup** — $350-$1,500: spreadsheet/CRM cleanup, SOP, templates, one simple automation.
4. **Authority Pack** — $400-$1,500: research-backed article, lead magnet, landing page, email sequence.
5. **Monthly Digital Care** — $250-$1,000/mo: website QA/updates, reporting, content updates, automation monitoring.

## Non-productized, case-by-case work

**Custom-quoted:** anything Tier C, and the Build/Retrofit/Run/Husks service lines (see `business.md`) — scoped per engagement, never off a fixed catalog price. Invoiced ad hoc, no Stripe product needed.

**Hourly consulting:** $85/hr list rate (adjustable-quantity Stripe link); the reference engagement (Swyft Market) ran a Phase 1 fixed-fee audit (5 hrs × $60) followed by ongoing T&M at Net-15. Rate flexibility exists for early/reference clients — don't treat $85 as rigid.

## Pricing logic that governs every quote

- Prices in the catalog are **starter ranges, not promises** — quote after inputs, page/item counts, integrations, revision limits, and acceptance tests are known.
- Phase the work on anything beyond Tier A: a small fixed-fee audit/discovery phase first, then Phase 2+ as T&M or milestone-fixed, scoped only after Phase 1 findings (this is the standing rule, not a Swyft-specific one-off).
- Fixed-price projects: 50% deposit / 40% delivery / 10% handoff split (from the business ops kit). T&M: state the rate and invoice cadence (weekly or bi-weekly, Net-15) up front.
- New scope discovered mid-engagement always goes through a change order — never silently absorbed into the existing SOW.
- Scope sections are ID'd (A1, A2, B1-B6...) so time logs, findings, progress reports, and invoice line items all reference the same IDs end to end.

## Claims to avoid (hard rules, enforced across every catalog category — not optional style guidance)

- Never guarantee rankings, sales, ad results, reviews, or deliverability.
- Never promise a production result from a single untested AI prompt.
- Never fabricate expertise, testimonials, or plagiarize; no academic cheating, no guaranteed outcomes on regulated content.
- Never clone a voice or likeness without documented, on-file permission.
- Never accept contracts, move money, impersonate the client, or send messages on their behalf without explicit approval.
- Never disable security controls, retain client credentials past the engagement, or touch regulated systems without a matching agreement and demonstrated expertise.
- Every factual, legal, financial, or medical claim requires client verification before it ships — Allternit states source, freshness, uncertainty, and validation method rather than asserting facts on the client's behalf.

## What counts as a good-fit customer

See `customer.md` for the full ICP. In short: has a real, nameable bottleneck or an existing-but-unclear digital asset (the Swyft pattern), is willing to fund a short paid discovery phase for anything beyond Tier A, and isn't asking for guaranteed results, credential access without an agreement, or regulated-data work without compliance clarity (any of those three are decline/re-scope signals per the kickoff playbook).
